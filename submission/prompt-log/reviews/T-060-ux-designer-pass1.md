# T-060 pass 1: enterprise UI/UX review (ux-designer-reviewer)

**Verdict: changes.** No blocker (no excerpt hidden, no export of unaccepted items, no pasted input lost). 15 majors, 11 minors. Scores: design quality 2, originality 2, craft 2, functionality 3. Each score of 2 is backed by majors F3 to F6 below.

Chris asked for a polished enterprise application, not a vibe-coded one. Judged against that bar, the product has the right ingredients (verbatim quotes in every row, side-by-side conflicts, a rail-style check stepper, separate letter and internal-notes exports) but it reads as several features styled by different hands on a debug-grade shell: three unrelated palettes, two primary blues, 21 type sizes, a catalogue of 15 buttons above the work, raw identifiers in the copy, a literal `null`, a stale error, and a dark mode that hides headings. Most of it is one token merge (F3) plus a header and a row component (F2, F4 to F6).

**Scope.** App at HEAD f187a30 (UI identical at 3223e50), http://127.0.0.1:7961, ZLAYT_MODE=local, fixture backend, sample case plus library cases lib01, lib06, lib09, lib11, lib14. Evidence: `artifacts/review-evidence/T-060-pass1/` (my files are prefixed `ux-`; the folder also holds files from other reviewers). Probe scripts and outputs are in the same folder.

**How I compared.** Each finding names the product or design system it is compared against. Carbon, Fluent 2, GOV.UK and WCAG rules are the ones opened in research/ui-references.md. For EvenUp, Supio, CaseMark, Filevine and Litify that file records marketing text only, so those comparisons are to the capability each page states, not to a screen I saw. Clio, DigitalOwl, Atlassian and Material comparisons are from general knowledge and are labelled as such.
